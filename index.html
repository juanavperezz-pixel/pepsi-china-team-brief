01 / Case in one minute

What is the actual problem?

Lay’s was growing in China, but PepsiCo could not consistently source potatoes suitable for chips. Large national potato harvests did not automatically translate into usable industrial supply.

Working problem statement — for team discussion

PepsiCo must create a reliable, scalable supply of chip-grade potatoes in China while deciding how much production to own and how much to develop through local suppliers.

This is our analytical framing, not a sentence copied from the case. Keep the submitted version to four lines or fewer.
01

Demand was outgrowing supply

Lay’s launched in 1997. A 2,000-tonne purchase supported only about three months of Shanghai retail sales. A Lanzhou launch was repeatedly postponed by shortages.

Case pp. 6, 8
02

Quality was the bottleneck

Chip production required particular shape, sugar, moisture and solid-content characteristics. Small farms, limited mechanization and uneven logistics made dependable supply difficult.

Case pp. 2, 8–10
03

Control created new risks

PepsiCo built a demonstration farm, experienced a costly crop failure with a U.S. partner, and later managed the farm locally while developing contracted growers.

Case pp. 8–10
What should we compare in our paper? (optional)
Owned farms

More direct quality and scheduling control; larger investment and farming responsibilities.

Contract growers

Lower direct asset ownership; more dependence on farmer capabilities, incentives and monitoring.

Hybrid approach

Maintain some controlled production as a learning and buffer capability while scaling qualified contracted growers; requires coordination.

These are possible strategic alternatives, not outcomes proven by the case. Evaluate them with the full licensed reading.
